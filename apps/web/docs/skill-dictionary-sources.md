# Skill dictionary: sources, licences, changes

`lib/scoring/data/skills.json` is a static skill dictionary that the keyword check consults behind the curated
taxonomy in `lib/scoring/taxonomy.ts`. It is generated by hand on a developer machine and committed; nothing is
downloaded at install, build or run time.

Retrieved and checked on 2026-10-06.

## Sources

### ESCO (European Commission)

- What: ESCO v1.2.1 skills pillar, 13,939 concepts, English and German preferred and alternative labels.
  ESCO has no Turkish labels.
- How: the download portal (<https://esco.ec.europa.eu/en/use-esco/download>) sends CSV packages by email only,
  so `scripts/fetch-esco-skills.mjs` reads the same released data from the public ESCO web service
  (<https://ec.europa.eu/esco/api/search>, `selectedVersion=v1.2.1`).
- Licence: Commission Decision 2011/833/EU on the reuse of Commission documents; EU content is under CC BY 4.0
  (<https://commission.europa.eu/legal-notice_en>, <https://esco.ec.europa.eu/en/copyright-notice-esco-skills-competences>).
- Required statement (ESCO FAQ, <https://esco.ec.europa.eu/en/about-esco/faq?page=2>): for services, tools and
  applications: "This service uses the ESCO classification of the European Commission." Any modified or adapted
  version of ESCO must be clearly indicated as such.
- Third-party parts: the ESCO copyright notice credits O*NET, the Canadian skills checklist, and Bertelsmann
  Stiftung cards (CC BY-SA 4.0) used for the transversal skills hierarchy. Transversal concepts are excluded
  from the dictionary.

### O*NET (U.S. Department of Labor, Employment and Training Administration)

- What: O*NET 31.0 database, `software_skills.csv` (the technology examples, with the Hot Technology and In Demand
  flags), from <https://www.onetcenter.org/database.html>.
- Licence: CC BY 4.0 (<https://www.onetcenter.org/license_db.html>,
  <https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/>).
- Required credit, modified form (from the O*NET "How to Credit Us" section): "This page includes information
  from the O*NET 31.0 Database by the U.S. Department of Labor, Employment and Training Administration
  (USDOL/ETA). Used under the CC BY 4.0 license. O*NET® is a trademark of USDOL/ETA. [Your name or company] has
  modified all or some of this information. USDOL/ETA has not approved, endorsed, or tested these
  modifications." The ® must be shown.

### manual-tr

- `scripts/skill-sources/manual-tr.tsv`, written for this project: Turkish CV skills, tools, methods and
  certificates, each with an English cross-reference. No attribution needed.

## Where the credit is shown

The about page (`app/[locale]/about/page.tsx`, section "Data sources") shows the ESCO statement and the O*NET
credit verbatim in all three locales (`about.escoCredit`, `about.onetCredit`), with a translated note that both
sources were filtered and shortened, linking here.

## What was changed (the modifications the credits refer to)

From ESCO:

- Only released concepts of type "knowledge" linked to at least one occupation; skill and competence concepts
  (verb phrases such as "manage budgets") and transversal concepts are dropped.
- Labels are lowercased, parenthetical qualifiers removed ("Java (computer programming)" becomes "java").
- Dropped: labels over four words or 48 characters, labels with commas or other punctuation, phrases with an
  interior function word ("types of pallets", "methoden der akupunktur"), catalogue headings ("Arten ...",
  "Eigenschaften ..."), and a reviewed list of everyday phrases ("body language", "application process").
- Single English words are kept only if they are product-shaped (internal capital, digit, symbol, acronym) or a
  proper noun ESCO never uses as an ordinary lowercase word. Single German words are kept only if they are the
  compound of a multi-word English concept and end on a discipline or method head ("Risikomanagement").
- An English one-word alternative label of a multi-word concept is kept only if it is the concept's acronym.

From O*NET:

- Only technology examples flagged Hot Technology or In Demand, or listed for at least five occupations.
- A trailing "software" is dropped. A vendor-less alias is added where it is product-shaped
  ("Autodesk AutoCAD" gets "autocad"; "Microsoft Teams" does not get "teams").

For every source:

- No single word that is a stopword, job-ad noise, an ambiguous token (`lib/scoring/ambiguous-terms.ts`) or on
  the reviewed generic list in the build script; no phrase that ends on a stopword.
- At most four aliases per entry; one owner per spelling (hand-made list first, then ESCO, then O*NET).
- At runtime the curated taxonomy and its synonyms win: a dictionary entry whose spelling the curated table
  already uses is ignored.

## Rebuilding

```bash
cd apps/web
node scripts/fetch-esco-skills.mjs v1.2.1          # writes .skill-sources/esco-v1.2.1/
mkdir -p .skill-sources/onet-31.0
curl -o .skill-sources/onet-31.0/software_skills.csv \
  https://www.onetcenter.org/dl_files/database/db_31_0_csv/software_skills.csv
node scripts/build-skill-dictionary.mjs             # writes lib/scoring/data/skills.json
```

`.skill-sources/` is git-ignored. The build prints entry counts, the gzip size (budget 300 KB) and a sample of
every rejection reason; read them before committing a rebuild. When the O*NET version changes, update the
version in the build script and in `about.onetCredit` (tests/skill-dictionary.test.ts checks they agree), and
bump `ENGINE_VERSION`.
